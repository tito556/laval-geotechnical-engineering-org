Geotechnical Engineering in Laval

Together, we solve the challenges of tomorrow.

LEARN MORE →

In Laval, what you see at the surface almost never matches what lies beneath. The island's geology is dominated by sensitive Champlain Sea clay deposits that can lose up to 90% of their strength when disturbed — a condition that has triggered costly slope failures across the St. Lawrence Lowlands. Our team runs a complete soil mechanics study using both in-situ testing and laboratory analysis to quantify that risk before excavation begins. We correlate field data from SPT drilling with advanced lab triaxial tests to model how the soil will behave under the actual structural load, not just under textbook assumptions. More than 440,000 people live and work on this island, and every new project — whether a six-storey condo in Chomedey or a warehouse extension in the industrial park along Autoroute 440 — depends on one thing: knowing exactly what the ground can carry.

Sensitive Champlain clay loses most of its shear strength when remolded — a fact that makes undisturbed sampling and careful lab handling the difference between a safe foundation and a geotechnical surprise.

Methodology and scope

Laval sits at a mean elevation of roughly 40 meters above sea level, but the depth to competent bedrock varies dramatically — from less than 2 meters in the eastern sectors near Saint-François to over 30 meters in the central clay plains. A proper soil mechanics study here must map that variability. We run a full suite of index and mechanical tests: grain-size distribution per ASTM D422, Atterberg limits to identify the sensitive clay fraction, consolidated-undrained triaxial compression, and Proctor compaction curves for engineered fill. The laboratory operates under ISO/IEC 17025 accreditation, so every data point that goes into your foundation report is defensible under third-party review. For sites where clay sensitivity raises red flags, we often combine the soil mechanics program with a liquefaction assessment to check whether the fine-grained layers might exhibit cyclic softening under the seismic loads prescribed by NBCC 2020. It is an extra step, but on Île Jésus the cost of skipping it can run into mid-six-figure remediation bills after a single wet construction season.

Local geotechnical context

While NBCC 2020 and CSA A23.3 provide the foundational structural design requirements, Laval's unique seismic conditions and sensitive clay deposits necessitate geotechnical assessments that go far beyond standard bearing capacity calculations. Differential settlement poses the most significant threat; a structure resting on both dense till and soft clay will inevitably tilt unless the soil mechanics study pinpoints the transition zone and the structural design adapts accordingly. Another frequent issue is slope failure during excavations, particularly in Sainte-Dorothée and Laval-Ouest, where natural drainage channels intersect the clay plain. To address these challenges, an extensive program involving undisturbed sampling, triaxial testing, and consolidation analysis equips the design team with the necessary drained and undrained parameters before any excavation begins.

Associated technical services

01

Foundation Design Soil Package

We provide a full suite of index tests (grain size, Atterberg limits, natural water content) along with mechanical properties derived from triaxial and consolidation testing. The output includes allowable bearing pressure, estimated total and differential settlement, and modulus of subgrade reaction, all presented in a format ready for structural engineers to apply under NBCC 2020.

02

Excavation & Slope Stability Support

Our analysis concentrates on the drained and undrained shear strengths of the Champlain clay formation. It encompasses multi-stage triaxial tests, sensitivity assessments, and stability evaluations that determine safe excavation slopes, dewatering needs, and required setbacks from adjacent buildings.

03

Compaction Control & Engineered Fill

We conduct both standard and modified Proctor compaction tests alongside field sand-cone density checks after placement. This ensures that the fill material aligns with the moisture content and density assumptions used in the soil mechanics study, bridging laboratory predictions with onsite conditions.

Regulatory framework

Our work adheres to NBCC 2020 (covering seismic and foundation requirements), CSA A23.3:2019 (geotechnical input for concrete design), and key ASTM methods: D422-63(2007) for particle-size analysis, D4318-17e1 for Atterberg limits, D4767-11(2020) for consolidated undrained triaxial compression, and D2435/D2435M-11(2020) for one-dimensional consolidation. Laboratory testing is conducted under ISO/IEC 17025:2017 accreditation.

Questions and answers

How long does a soil mechanics study take for a typical building lot in Laval?

Field operations for a typical residential or light-commercial site—including drilling, sampling, and standard penetration tests—take one to two days. The subsequent laboratory phase requires 10 to 15 business days, varying with the volume of consolidation and triaxial tests. An interpretative report containing foundation recommendations is generally issued three to four weeks following the completion of fieldwork.

What is the approximate cost range for a soil mechanics study in Laval?

In Laval, costs for projects like single-family homes, townhouse clusters, or small commercial buildings—covering drilling, certified laboratory analyses, and the engineering report—range from CA$4,150 to CA$7,160. The exact amount depends on borehole quantity, refusal depth, and the particular tests requested by the structural engineer.

Why is Champlain Sea clay such a concern on Île Jésus?

The Champlain Sea clay, formed about 10,000 years ago in a post-glacial marine setting, derives its flocculated structure from salt bonds. Leaching by fresh water or mechanical remolding during excavation can cause it to liquefy. Because of this sensitivity, any geotechnical investigation must incorporate meticulous undisturbed sampling and sensitivity testing; failing to do so risks using strength parameters that are not representative during construction.

Does the soil mechanics study include a seismic site classification for NBCC?

Absolutely. By measuring or estimating the average shear-wave velocity within the top 30 meters (Vs30), we determine the appropriate NBCC Site Class—usually C, D, or E in Laval based on clay depth. This classification directly impacts the design spectrum and can substantially affect the seismic base shear that a structure must withstand.
