Geotechnical Excavation Monitoring in Laval: Real-Time Control That Keeps Your Project on Schedule

Together, we solve the challenges of tomorrow.

LEARN MORE →

With over 440,000 residents and a construction boom reshaping the island, Laval's excavation projects face a unique challenge: dense urban spacing and sensitive clay deposits that demand millimeter-level precision. Our monitoring systems track lateral movement, pore pressure, and vibration continuously, giving the site team live data instead of after-the-fact reports. Whether the dig is next to a 1960s school on Boulevard des Laurentides or a new condo tower near the Rivière des Prairies, the instrumentation package adapts to the risk profile of the structure. For deeper cuts where soil behavior changes fast, we often pair the monitoring array with a CPT test to calibrate the stratigraphy against real-time deformation readings, ensuring the shoring design matches what the ground actually does under load.

Real-time deformation data turns a reactive dig into a controlled operation where threshold alerts trigger decisions, not emergencies.

Methodology and scope

Laval sits on a mix of Champlain Sea clays and till, which react dramatically to seasonal freeze-thaw cycles and spring runoff. A monitoring plan here has to account for stiff overconsolidated crust in summer turning into a water-sensitive paste after a single rain event. Our approach layers automated total stations with in-place inclinometers and vibrating-wire piezometers, capturing tilt, settlement, and pore pressure simultaneously. The data feeds into a cloud dashboard that flags threshold breaches within 60 seconds, not the usual 24 hours. This speed matters when a retaining wall on Autoroute 15 exhibits unexpected drift at 3 a.m. — the engineer can assess the trend, compare it against the baseline model, and issue a hold-or-proceed call without driving to the site. The system also logs temperature and vibration, which becomes critical when blasting or pile driving occurs within 50 meters of heritage masonry.

Local geotechnical context

In Laval, a mixed-use building with 14 stories on a constrained site near Boulevard Saint-Martin required shoring anchors that extended under the neighboring fire station. The general contractor expected the soldier pile wall to experience less than 15 mm of deflection. But when subgrade was reached after three days, the inclinometer at mid-span recorded 22 mm of movement, and the crack gauge on the fire station's east wall showed a 0.8 mm opening. Because the monitoring system triggered an alert at the 18 mm threshold, excavation was stopped and compensation grouting was injected behind the wall, preventing crack propagation. Without that early warning, the city would have evacuated the station and imposed a stop-work order for weeks. In Laval's dense urban environment, there is little room for error: a single excavation without monitoring can lead to third-party claims that exceed the project's contingency in just one afternoon.

Associated technical services

01

Inclinometer & Tilt Monitoring

To measure lateral deformation against depth, both in-place and manual inclinometer arrays are placed within shoring walls or in the surrounding soil. The collected data is then correlated with the stage of excavation and the groundwater conditions.

02

Settlement & Crack Monitoring

Baseline condition surveys capture the pre-construction state, and then precision level surveys are conducted on nearby buildings, sidewalks, and utilities, with crack gauges added on vulnerable structures.

03

Pore Pressure & Groundwater Control

Verification of dewatering system effectiveness and detection of any pressure accumulation behind the shoring is done using vibrating-wire piezometers and observation wells.

04

Vibration & Noise Compliance

For activities like blasting, pile driving, or compaction near hospitals and occupied buildings, triaxial geophones and sound level meters are deployed in accordance with CNESST requirements.

Regulatory framework

Applicable standards include ASTM D7299-12 for inclinometer verification, NBCC 2015 Part 4 for excavation safety, CAN/CSA-S6-14 for highway structures monitoring, ASTM D6230 for automated geotechnical monitoring, and CNESST vibration guidelines.

Questions and answers

How much does geotechnical excavation monitoring cost for a typical Laval project?

In Laval, a typical basement excavation that includes inclinometers, settlement points, and a piezometer typically has a budget ranging from CA$1,200 to CA$3,760, which varies based on the number of instruments, how long monitoring lasts, and how often reports are issued. While a fixed scope with automated alerts requires a higher initial investment, it frequently eliminates weeks of manual survey work.

What triggers an alert, and who receives it?

Alerts are triggered at three thresholds: 70%, 85%, and 95% of the design deformation or vibration limit. Upon crossing any threshold, the monitoring system simultaneously sends an SMS and email to the superintendent, geotechnical engineer, and shoring designer. Each alert contains the sensor ID, the current measured value, the rate of change, and a plot covering the previous 48 hours, enabling the team to make an immediate decision.

Do you provide the monitoring report for the city and the contractor's insurance?

Indeed, each project is provided with a pre-construction baseline report, data summaries (daily or weekly) throughout excavation, and a final close-out report. This documentation complies with the specifications of Laval's urban planning department and is recognized by most liability insurers as evidence of ongoing monitoring.
