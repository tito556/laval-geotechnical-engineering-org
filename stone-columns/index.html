Stone Column Design in Laval: Ground Improvement for Compressible Soils

Together, we solve the challenges of tomorrow.

LEARN MORE →

We see it repeatedly in Laval: a contractor assumes standard footings will work on a site near Rivière des Mille Îles, only to hit soft, compressible clay at 3 meters. The budget blows up when differential settlement appears before the structure is even closed in. Stone column design eliminates that risk by creating stiff, load-bearing columns through the weak zone, transferring stress to competent till or bedrock below. Our laboratory handles the full design sequence—from index testing on Shelby tube samples to verifying column modulus with post-installation load tests. For Laval’s Champlain Sea clays, which cover much of the island and can exceed 20 meters in thickness, a properly executed CPT test before design gives us the continuous profile needed to size diameter, spacing, and depth correctly, avoiding the guesswork that leads to underperformance.

A stone column in Laval’s sensitive clay is not just a load path—it is a drain. The column’s permeability, typically 10⁻³ to 10⁻² cm/s, accelerates consolidation from decades to months.

Methodology and scope

A recent project in the Chomedey industrial sector illustrates the process. The site had 9 meters of soft grey clay over dense till. The developer needed a floor slab supporting 40 kPa live load with no more than 25 mm total settlement. We began with undisturbed sampling to run triaxial consolidated-undrained tests per ASTM D4767, establishing the clay’s effective friction angle and undrained shear strength profile. The stone column design used a triangular grid at 2.1-meter spacing, columns 0.8 meters in diameter installed by wet top-feed vibroflot. Our lab verified aggregate gradation against ASTM D448 No. 57 stone specifications before installation. During construction, we ran Proctor tests on the gravel backfill and checked compaction of the load transfer platform—a granular mattress 0.6 meters thick that distributes slab loads evenly to the column heads. Post-installation, plate load tests on three columns confirmed a modulus exceeding 25 MPa, well above the design requirement. That kind of end-to-end verification separates a reliable ground improvement job from one that fails silently under service loads.

Local geotechnical context

The geology of Laval is divided into two distinct zones. In the western plateau near Sainte-Dorothée, glacial till and shallow bedrock are present, making stone columns uncommon because adequate bearing capacity is achieved within 2 meters. Moving east to Duvernay or Saint-François, the Champlain Sea basin takes over, characterized by sensitive silty clays. This creates a sharp contrast: one site may require only a compaction test for granular fill, while another might necessitate 15-meter stone columns designed to control settlement under a six-story residential building. A frequent error is implementing a standard column grid without considering Laval’s clay sensitivity. The remolded strength can fall below 20% of the peak value, making installation sequencing crucial—incorrect spacing leads to pore pressure buildup in adjacent columns, reducing effective confinement. Under NBCC 2015 seismic demands for the Montreal-Laval region, these soils also have liquefaction potential. Stone columns serve a dual purpose here: they reduce settlement and act as drainage paths to prevent excessive pore pressure during seismic events.

Associated technical services

01

Stone Column Design Package

The deliverable includes geotechnical site characterization, column geometry (diameter, spacing, depth), settlement analysis via the Priebe method, liquefaction assessment per Youd-Idriss 2001 using SPT or CPT data, aggregate specifications, and installation sequence. It is provided as a stamped report that meets requirements for Laval municipal permit applications.

02

Installation Verification & Load Testing

Our services cover on-site monitoring during vibro-replacement, verification of aggregate gradation, and post-installation plate load tests per ASTM D1194. Column modulus is measured and settlement performance is confirmed against design predictions. Documentation is supplied for the engineer of record and the city of Laval.

Regulatory framework

Relevant standards include ASTM D4767-11 (Consolidated-Undrained Triaxial Compression Test), NBCC 2015 (National Building Code of Canada) seismic hazard for Laval, FHWA-NHI-10-016 (Ground Improvement Methods for Stone Columns), ASTM D448-12 (Standard Classification for Aggregate Sizes), and BNQ 2501-092 (Quebec standards for granular backfill).

Questions and answers

What does stone column design cost for a typical Laval site?

For a standard design package covering one building footprint in Laval, the fee typically ranges from CA$1.930 to CA$7.910, depending on the number of columns, depth, and whether CPT or SPT data is already available. Projects needing additional triaxial testing or liquefaction analysis are closer to the upper end.

How deep do stone columns need to go in Laval's clay deposits?

In Laval, most stone columns are terminated between 9 and 25 meters deep, reaching the dense till or bedrock beneath the Champlain Sea clay. The exact depth is determined by the compressible layer thickness and applied load. Bearing strata are identified using CPT refusal data or SPT N-values exceeding 50 blows per foot.

Can stone columns prevent liquefaction under Laval's seismic conditions?

Yes, this is a key advantage in the Montreal-Laval seismic zone. Stone columns act as vertical drains, allowing rapid dissipation of excess pore pressure during shaking. With spacing of 3 meters or less, these columns can reduce liquefaction potential in granular interbeds within the clay profile—relevant for Laval sites classified as Site Class D or E under NBCC 2015.
