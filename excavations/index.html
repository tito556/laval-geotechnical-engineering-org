Underground Excavations in Laval

Together, we solve the challenges of tomorrow.

LEARN MORE →

In Laval, the demand for underground structures like parking garages, water storage, and transit tunnels has grown significantly as the city expands, especially in central districts such as Chomedey and Pont-Viau. This field is a vital part of geotechnical engineering, involving the planning, execution, and reinforcement of underground spaces for utilities, transport, and infrastructure. It covers the entire process of constructing safe and stable voids below ground, dealing with hazards from soil movement, water pressure, and the safeguarding of nearby buildings. For civil contractors and project developers, a thorough strategy for subsurface work is essential, not just advisable, to ensure project success and public safety in Laval's intricate urban setting.

Any underground project in Laval must contend with complicated local geology. The city lies in the St. Lawrence Lowlands, underlain chiefly by the Lorraine Group bedrock composed of shale, siltstone, and sandstone. Notably, this bedrock is frequently highly weathered and fractured near the rockhead, covered by a thick layer of glacial till and sensitive Champlain Sea clays. Those clays are well-known for their unstable structure; excavation can trigger abrupt strength loss, resulting in large retrogressive landslides or substantial settlements. Adding to the complexity, the bedrock surface is irregular and confined aquifers exist within the till. Therefore, an exhaustive geotechnical study is mandatory before any deep excavation can commence.

All underground excavation work in Laval is governed by a strict regulatory framework. At the provincial level, the Quebec Construction Code (Chapter I, Building) and the Safety Code for the Construction Industry (SST) are paramount, with the latter mandating specific shoring and protection systems for trenches and excavations deeper than 1.2 meters. The professional framework is set by the Engineers Act, requiring that the geotechnical design of deep excavations be performed and sealed by an engineer registered with the Ordre des ingénieurs du Québec (OIQ). Municipal permits from the Ville de Laval are required for dewatering and shoring, and projects must often comply with environmental guidelines from the MELCCFP, particularly for groundwater management and the disposal of excavated soils containing naturally occurring contaminants.

The types of projects driving demand for underground excavation expertise in Laval are diverse and growing. Major urban infrastructure is at the forefront, including the construction of deep interceptor sewers and the extension of the Montreal Metro's Orange Line, which requires station caverns and running tunnels excavated through mixed-face conditions. In the private sector, mid-rise and high-rise residential developments in Sainte-Dorothée and Duvernay routinely require multi-level underground parking, demanding robust temporary support of excavation systems like tied-back soldier pile walls or secant piles. Industrial projects, such as underground storage for snow melters or water treatment plant expansions, also rely on advanced geotechnical excavation monitoring to verify design assumptions and ensure the safety of workers and nearby infrastructure throughout the construction phase.

Questions and answers

What are the main geotechnical risks associated with underground excavations in Laval?

Risks in Laval's underground work mainly arise from the geology. Sensitive Champlain Sea clays are prone to causing major retrogressive landslides when disturbed. Additionally, the bedrock is highly fractured and weathered, leading to stability issues and water ingress. Artesian aquifers in the glacial till can induce basal heave. Dewatering or ground loss may also cause settlement damage to adjacent buildings and utilities.

What regulations govern the design and safety of underground excavations in Quebec?

Local regulations in Laval require permits for dewatering, shoring, and soil disposal, typically demanding adherence to provincial environmental rules. At the broader level, underground excavations fall under the Quebec Construction Code and the Safety Code for the Construction Industry (SST). According to the SST, protective systems are compulsory for any excavation deeper than 1.2 meters. Moreover, the design must be certified by an engineer registered with the OIQ.

How is the stability of adjacent buildings ensured during a deep excavation in Laval?

A three-step approach guarantees stability. First, a comprehensive monitoring program uses instruments like inclinometers, settlement points, and vibration monitors on nearby structures to supply real-time information. If movements surpass set limits, the design is promptly modified. Second, a robust support system such as secant or diaphragm walls minimizes ground displacement. Third, a predictive geotechnical analysis employing the observational method is conducted.

What is the typical difference between designing an excavation in rock versus soil in Laval?

For soil excavations in the overburden, especially in Champlain Sea clay, soil-structure interaction controls the design. This necessitates heavy, frequently pre-stressed shoring walls to manage lateral deformation and basal heave, with groundwater controlled via dewatering or cut-off walls. In rock excavations within the Lorraine Group shale, rock mass classification systems guide the design of rock bolts and shotcrete, with a primary concern being water inflow through fractures.
