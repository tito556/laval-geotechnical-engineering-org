MASW & VS30 Shear Wave Velocity Testing in Laval, QC

Laval sits at a modest 91 meters above sea level, yet the real variation that matters for seismic safety lies in its subsurface. The 1988 Saguenay earthquake—magnitude 5.9, felt strongly here—reminded engineers that eastern Canada's intraplate seismicity cannot be dismissed. On Île Jésus, post-glacial Champlain Sea clays blanket large sectors, while compact glacial till emerges near the Rivière des Mille Îles. A standard borehole alone won't give you the continuous shear wave velocity profile you need for NBCC 2020 site classification. That's where MASW (Multichannel Analysis of Surface Waves) comes in: a non-invasive survey that maps Vs30 across the site, pinpointing transitions from soft clay to stiffer lodgement till. When the stratigraphy is erratic—and in Laval, it often is—combining MASW with a seismic microzonation study clarifies how basin-edge effects might amplify ground motion in the western districts, or how a buried esker ridge under Chomedey changes the site period.

Champlain Sea clay in Laval can shear at strains below 1%, making Vs30 the critical parameter for avoiding Class E penalties in the structural design.

Method and coverage

Compare the terrain in Sainte-Dorothée with that in Duvernay and the difference is stark. Sainte-Dorothée sits on thick, sensitive marine silts that lose strength when disturbed; Vs30 values there can dip below 180 m/s, pushing the site toward Class E. Duvernay, by contrast, rests on a shallow till veneer over limestone bedrock, producing Vs30 readings often above 400 m/s—a firm Class C profile. MASW lets you map this lateral change without trenching or drilling across the property. The method uses a 24- or 48-channel seismic cable, a sledgehammer or weight-drop source, and inversion software that derives a 1D shear wave velocity column from the dispersion curve. Because the survey is surface-based, access is straightforward even on congested industrial lots in Laval's Autoroute 440 corridor. For sites where deeper bedrock confirmation is needed, we pair the surface wave data with a seismic refraction line to resolve the top-of-rock geometry, and where pile design depends on lateral soil stiffness, a CPT test provides continuous tip resistance and sleeve friction to calibrate the velocity profile against in-situ strength.

Local geotechnical context

NBCC 2020 Table 4.1.8.4.A ties the site amplification factor F(T) directly to Vs30. In Laval, where sensitive clay can shear at strains below 1%, misclassifying a Class E site as Class C inflates the short-period spectral acceleration by up to 40%—an error that translates into under-designed lateral systems. The city's east-west gradient, from rock-controlled till in the east to thick Champlain Sea silts in the west, means that two buildings 500 meters apart can have fundamentally different seismic demands. A single borehole every 50 meters won't catch that transition; MASW profiles spaced at 10–15 meters will. The risk isn't just regulatory—it's financial. A late-stage redesign after the excavation reveals unexpected soft clay costs multiples of the original geophysics budget. For critical infrastructure in Laval's industrial parks, we recommend coupling the MASW survey with a liquefaction assessment using the NCEER/Youd-Idriss procedure whenever the water table is within 5 meters of the surface and the fines content exceeds 35%.

Complementary services

01

Vs30 Site Classification Package

A signed NBCC 2020 site class letter is derived from three to five MASW lines using a 24-channel array, dispersion analysis, and 2D Vs cross-sections. This survey is appropriate for Part 9 buildings and mid-rise residential projects on Laval's till uplands.

02

Combined MASW + CPT + Refraction

For deep soft-clay sites west of Autoroute 13, an integrated approach of geophysical and penetration testing is utilized. The Vs profile is calibrated through CPT tip resistance and pore pressure measurements, while seismic refraction determines bedrock depth. This method is optimal for hospitals, schools, and post-disaster buildings.

03

Microzonation & Basin-Edge Study

Lateral velocity contrasts responsible for basin-edge amplification are mapped using a high-density MASW grid (10 m spacing) combined with HVSR stations. This setup is applied to campus-scale developments and infrastructure corridors crossing the Chomedey–Fabreville transition.

Regulatory framework

Relevant standards include NBCC 2020 Part 4 for seismic site classification, ASTM D4428/D7400 for crosshole and surface wave testing, CSA A23.3-19 for concrete structure seismic provisions, and NCEER-97-0001 for Youd-Idriss liquefaction triggering assessment.

Questions and answers

What does a MASW survey cost for a typical Laval residential lot?

The cost of a MASW survey for a single-family or duplex lot on Île Jésus ranges from CA$2,070 to CA$4,620. This fee includes two to three array positions and a signed NBCC site-class letter, with variations depending on access conditions and the need for supplementary seismic refraction lines to confirm bedrock depth.

How does MASW handle the Champlain Sea clay that is common in Laval?

The strong velocity contrast between Champlain Sea clay and underlying till or bedrock enables clear identification of the interface on Rayleigh-wave dispersion curves. However, the clay's low shear modulus at small strains requires a 2-Hz geophone array and an accelerated weight drop source to generate sufficient low-frequency energy for resolving the full 30-m column. Inversion is constrained by available borehole logs to address non-uniqueness in soft-soil profiles.

Is MASW accepted by Laval building officials for seismic site classification?

Affirmative. The City of Laval's building department accepts MASW-derived Vs30 values for NBCC 2020 site classification when the survey is directly supervised by a Professional Engineer licensed by the Ordre des ingénieurs du Québec. The report must include raw shot gathers, dispersion curves, and the inversion model, not just the final Vs30 figure. For Part 4 buildings, the engineering letter also references CSA A23.3-19 seismic provisions.
