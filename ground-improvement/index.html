Ground improvement in Laval

Together, we solve the challenges of tomorrow.

LEARN MORE →

Laval's ground improvement involves various geotechnical methods that modify and improve natural soil and fill properties, turning them into dependable foundation strata for building works. Located on Île Jésus, urban growth in Laval is greatly shaped by the Quaternary geology of the area, which features deep layers of sensitive marine clays and loose silty soils that create major obstacles. This field tackles the essential requirements of controlling settlement, boosting bearing capacity, and addressing liquefaction risks, so that structures and infrastructure operate safely throughout their intended lifespan. Choosing the right technique, whether dynamic approaches or rigid inclusions, is a complicated choice based on a deep comprehension of the local geotechnical environment.

The island's subsurface is predominantly characterized by the Champlain Sea clay, a well-known sensitive marine deposit that can lose significant strength when disturbed. Overlying this clay, or interspersed with it, are often loose to compact sand and silt layers, as well as organic soils in former low-lying areas. These conditions mean that traditional shallow foundations are frequently inadequate without prior treatment. The water table is typically high, and the presence of these soft, compressible soils creates risks of excessive total and differential settlement, slope instability, and in the case of loose granular layers, earthquake-induced liquefaction. A professional vibrocompaction design is a direct response to the latter risk, specifically targeting loose sands to increase their density and cyclic resistance.

Demonstration video

All ground improvement works in Laval must conform to the rigorous framework of Quebec's construction standards, primarily the Code de construction du Québec, which adopts the National Building Code of Canada with provincial amendments. Geotechnical site investigations are mandated by the Canadian Standards Association's CSA A23.3 for concrete design and the Canadian Foundation Engineering Manual (CFEM) principles, which guide the design and verification of improved ground. For dynamic methods and deep mixing, adherence to the Loi sur la qualité de l’environnement is essential to manage noise, vibration, and groundwater impacts. A design engineer registered with the Ordre des ingénieurs du Québec (OIQ) must seal all plans, ensuring that the proposed solution is compatible with the specific geotechnical risks identified in the site's vibrocompaction design or other improvement strategy.

The demand for ground improvement in Laval is driven by a diverse range of projects. High-density residential and commercial towers along the branches of the Metro and near Montmorency require stiff, settlement-free ground to support heavy column loads. Large-scale industrial and warehouse developments in areas like the Autoroute 440 corridor often encounter variable fill and soft soils, making ground treatment a prerequisite for slab-on-grade performance. Critical infrastructure projects, including bridge approaches, highway embankments, and water retention structures, rely on these techniques to ensure long-term stability and serviceability. For instance, a custom vibrocompaction design might be the core solution for a new logistics hub built over a loose alluvial sand deposit, while deep soil mixing could stabilize an excavation in sensitive clay for a municipal pump station.

Questions and answers

What is the main purpose of ground improvement in Laval's soil conditions?

The main goal is to reduce the dangers from the sensitive Champlain Sea clays and loose granular materials found locally. These methods aim to enhance bearing capacity, limit overall and uneven settlement, and remove liquefaction potential during earthquakes, thereby converting inherently poor soils into a secure and dependable foundation layer.

How do I know if my project in Laval requires a ground improvement solution?

A thorough geotechnical study determines the need, identifying troublesome soils such as soft clay, loose sand, or organic strata. When the assessment indicates that anticipated settlements surpass acceptable limits or that the ground's load-bearing capacity is inadequate for the intended structural forces, a ground improvement approach, rather than only deep foundations, is suggested.

What is the typical regulatory approval process for ground improvement works in Laval?

The procedure demands a design stamped by an engineer registered with the Ordre des ingénieurs du Québec (OIQ). This design needs to adhere to the Quebec Construction Code and applicable CSA specifications. Based on the technique employed, environmental permits concerning vibration, noise, and groundwater handling according to the provincial Environment Quality Act could also be needed prior to commencing work.

How is the performance of a ground improvement treatment verified on a Laval site?

Verification of performance occurs via in-situ testing both before, during, and after the treatment process. Typical approaches involve cone penetration testing (CPT) and standard penetration testing (SPT) to assess rises in tip resistance and density. Additionally, monitoring settlement after treatment with accurate surveying, along with load tests on trial pads or actual construction elements, is a common procedure to ensure that design requirements are fulfilled.
