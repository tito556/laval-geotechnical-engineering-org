Geotechnical Design of Deep Excavations in Laval: NBCC Compliance and Local Ground Conditions

Together, we solve the challenges of tomorrow.

LEARN MORE →

The National Building Code of Canada (NBCC 2020) sets strict requirements for deep excavation support, and in Laval, those requirements intersect with a unique geological profile. The city sits on Île Jésus, where the Rivière des Prairies and Rivière des Mille Îles have shaped a subsurface dominated by sensitive Champlain Sea clay. This marine clay, deposited over 10,000 years ago, can lose significant strength when disturbed. That is why geotechnical design here cannot rely on textbook assumptions. In our experience, a design that works in the Montreal bedrock core fails in Laval unless the clay sensitivity is properly characterized. We integrate site-specific parameters from in-situ permeability testing and advanced lab programs to predict deformation before the first bucket of soil is removed.

Designing a shoring wall in Laval means respecting the memory of the Champlain Sea. Get the clay sensitivity wrong, and the excavation becomes a liability.

Method and coverage

Laval's urban fabric expanded rapidly after the 1960s, transforming farmland and wetlands into dense residential and commercial hubs. This history left a legacy of mixed fill, buried organic soils, and old watercourses now hidden beneath parking lots. When we design a deep excavation near Centre Laval or along the Autoroute 15 corridor, we often encounter these surprises. The original surface drainage patterns were altered, creating perched groundwater pockets that complicate dewatering. A reliable design must account for these anthropogenic layers. We usually recommend coupling the excavation support analysis with a slope stability assessment for adjacent slopes, especially where the cut approaches existing road embankments or aging infrastructure. Understanding the city's development history is not academic; it is a practical tool for avoiding construction delays.

Local geotechnical context

In Laval, the compact gray silt-clay typically exhibits an undrained shear strength of 30–80 kPa in the uppermost 10 m. Although this soil appears adequately strong on paper, its brittle nature poses a genuine risk of progressive failure for temporary cuts, especially when left open through a wet spring. Unexpected pore pressure increases behind shoring walls can result from groundwater recharge originating from the Rivière des Mille Îles. If engineers design using only drained parameters and ignore short-term undrained behavior, there is a risk of base heave and wall deflections surpassing serviceability limits. For instance, we have observed a 6 m excavation that moved laterally more than 50 mm simply because pre-construction dewatering was halted too early. In Laval, the construction sequence is an integral part of the design; the two are inseparable.

Complementary services

01

Shoring Wall Design

Our firm designs both anchored and cantilevered wall systems, including soldier piles with lagging, secant pile walls, and diaphragm walls. These are dimensioned to handle the lateral earth pressures and surcharge loads that are characteristic of Laval's congested urban corridors.

02

Dewatering and Groundwater Control

We engineer dewatering systems that control perched water tables and the substantial groundwater inflows often encountered close to Laval's rivers. This ensures excavation bases remain stable and hydraulic uplift is avoided.

03

Instrumentation and Monitoring Plans

Our monitoring specifications incorporate inclinometers and piezometers to measure wall deflection and pore water pressures. This enables the construction crew to confirm design assumptions as work progresses.

Regulatory framework

Applicable standards include the National Building Code of Canada (NBCC 2020), CSA A23.3:19 for concrete structure design, and ASTM D7181 for the consolidated drained triaxial compression test on soils.

Questions and answers

How does the Champlain Sea clay affect deep excavation design in Laval?

The clay is sensitive; remolding can cause it to lose as much as 80% of its original undisturbed strength. Consequently, our short-term stability analysis employs undrained shear strength parameters, and we mandate careful excavation and dewatering sequences to avoid disturbing the soil and losing strength during construction.

What is the typical cost range for designing a deep excavation support system in Laval?

For a standard commercial or mixed-use excavation in Laval, the geotechnical design package costs between CA$2,570 and CA$12,770. The price varies with shoring system complexity, how many nearby structures need protection, and excavation depth. A straightforward cantilever system for a 4 m deep cut is at the lower bound; a multi-level anchored wall with comprehensive instrumentation is at the upper end.

Which NBCC seismic site class applies to most Laval sites?

Based on shear wave velocity data and undrained shear strength profiles, the majority of sites situated on Champlain Sea clay deposits are classified as Site D or E according to NBCC 2020. This classification has a direct impact on the seismic earth pressures incorporated into our shoring wall designs.

Can a deep excavation be designed without pre-construction soil borings?

That is not correct. The NBCC mandates a geotechnical investigation for both permanent and temporary structures. In Laval, due to variable till thickness and the presence of hidden organic layers, boring and cone penetration testing are necessary to develop a dependable ground model for excavation design.
