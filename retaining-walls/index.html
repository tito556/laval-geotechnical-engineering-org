Laval's rapid transformation from a patchwork of rural parishes into Quebec's third-largest city has placed unprecedented demand on its subsurface. The island geography, framed by the Rivière des Mille Îles and Rivière des Prairies, dictates that much of the available land sits atop sensitive Champlain Sea silts and compact glacial till. For any project involving grade changes along Autoroute 15 or new foundations in Chomedey, a proper retaining wall design must reconcile ambitious architectural vision with the reality of a soil profile that can transition from stiff clay to loose sand in less than a meter. The engineering team tackles these interfaces daily, applying a methodology that begins with thorough site characterization before any geometry is drafted. Integrating a CPT test early in the investigation provides the continuous stratigraphic profile that conventional boreholes often miss in these transitional deposits.

In Laval's Champlain Sea deposits, lateral earth pressure on a retaining wall is not a constant—it evolves with the pore-water pressure regime that shifts between spring thaw and late-summer drought.

Methodology and scope

Compliance with the National Building Code of Canada and CSA A23.3 forms the backbone of every structural calculation, but in Laval, the geotechnical narrative is written by the soil's stress history. The heavily overconsolidated upper crust of the Champlain clay can support significant bearing pressure, yet its fissured fabric requires a conservative approach to long-term lateral earth pressure, particularly where seasonal moisture fluctuations in the top 3 meters create a dynamic shrink-swell envelope. We employ strain-compatibility analysis for cantilever walls and gravity structures, ensuring that the design does not simply meet minimum factors of safety but anticipates the gradual relaxation of horizontal stress that occurs in excavations left open through a Quebec winter. When bedrock is shallow in the western sectors, a complete understanding of the rock mass is essential; in many cases, the investigation is complemented with seismic refraction to map the top of the competent limestone and identify any karstic voids before finalizing the wall's heel geometry.

Local geotechnical context

A track-mounted hydraulic drill rig, featuring thin-walled Shelby tubes and an SPT auto-hammer calibrated per ASTM D1586 energy criteria, is deployed by the field crew. The rig's ability to navigate is challenged in the tight spaces behind Boulevard Saint-Martin's commercial strips, yet the sampling procedure ultimately determines success. Skilled operators push a 75 mm Shelby tube into Champlain clay at a constant rate of 2 cm per second, a technique honed through extensive core recovery across Laval. Rushing the sample introduces disturbance, which in turn leads to inaccurate preconsolidation pressure estimates—resulting in a retaining wall design that may be overly conservative or unsafely optimistic regarding lateral squeeze at the excavation base.

Associated technical services

01

Cantilever and Counterfort Wall Engineering

Overturning, sliding, and bearing analyses are conducted for reinforced concrete walls. To properly dimension the stem and base slab, we simulate the transition zone where the Champlain clay's rigid crust transitions into softer, normally consolidated material, employing limit equilibrium and finite element methodologies.

02

Anchored and Soil-Nailed Systems

Permanent and temporary tieback anchors are engineered for excavations deeper than 4 meters. The bond length of each anchor is determined using in-situ shear strength obtained from pressuremeter tests, and corrosion protection levels follow PTI guidelines to address the aggressive soil chemistry in certain pockets of Laval's former agricultural lands.

03

Segmental Block and MSE Walls

Geogrid-reinforced mechanically stabilized earth walls offer a practical option for commercial projects in Laval-sur-le-Lac. The design considers the native backfill's low pH and sulfate levels, choosing polymeric reinforcement with suitable long-term creep reduction factors.

04

Drainage and Filter Design

The effectiveness of a retaining wall hinges on its drainage system. To avoid hydrostatic pressure accumulation behind the wall—a frequent failure mode in older Laval structures lacking a continuous free-draining column—we specify graded filter blankets, chimney drains, and weep hole configurations.

Regulatory framework

Applicable code references include NBCC 2015 Part 4 for structural design, CSA A23.3:19 for concrete structures, ASTM D1586-18 for SPT testing, CAN/CSA-S6-19 for highway bridges, and ASTM D422-63 for particle-size analysis.

Questions and answers

What is the typical cost range for retaining wall design in Laval?

Retaining wall design engineering fees usually fall between CA$1,500 and CA$5,200, with variations based on wall height, soil profile complexity, and the need for additional studies such as CPT or triaxial testing. A straightforward gravity wall shorter than 1.2 meters would be on the lower side, whereas a fully anchored system with monitoring instrumentation would be at the higher end.

How does the Champlain Sea clay affect lateral earth pressure?

The upper crust of Champlain clay is heavily overconsolidated from glacial unloading and desiccation, leading to in-situ horizontal stress (K0) values that can exceed 1.0 near the surface. Excavating in front of the wall induces stress relief, potentially causing progressive failure along fissures. In our designs, we employ a depth-decaying K0 and consider the soil's strain-softening behavior, instead of generic Rankine values that understate the true wall load.

What seismic provisions apply to retaining walls in Laval?

According to NBCC 2015, Laval is in a moderate seismic hazard zone. For retaining walls exceeding 3 meters, we use the Mononobe-Okabe pseudo-static method to determine the seismic component of lateral earth pressure. The horizontal acceleration coefficient comes from the site-specific spectral response acceleration at 0.2 seconds, adjusted for Site Class D or E, common across much of the island.

Do you handle the municipal permit process for the wall?

For a Laval retaining wall, the engineering package comprises sealed calculations, structural drawings, and a geotechnical report outlining the soil parameters. It is formatted to comply with the submission guidelines of the Service de l'urbanisme de Laval. Although we do not handle submissions, the documentation is organized to ensure the permit review process faces no technical inquiries about foundation conditions.
