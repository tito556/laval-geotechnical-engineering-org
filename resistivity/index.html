Electrical Resistivity Imaging and VES Sounding in Laval

Together, we solve the challenges of tomorrow.

DETAILS →

A few months ago we were called out to a site off Boulevard Saint-Martin. The contractor was ready to dig but the borehole log showed a buried channel of till that didn't match the surrounding clay. We ran a VES line in under two hours and the resistivity profile lit up the channel boundaries clear as day. Laval's subsurface is like that—glacial clay, compact till, and limestone bedrock stacked in unpredictable layers. Electrical resistivity lets us see the contrasts without waiting for lab results. It works because the Champlain Sea clay is conductive while the till and limestone are resistive. When we combine VES soundings with a few SPT boreholes, we anchor the resistivity values to actual blow counts, giving the structural engineer a continuous cross-section instead of just point data.

A resistivity contrast of 10 ohm-m versus 100 ohm-m is often the difference between stable till and sensitive clay in the Champlain Sea basin.

Method and coverage

Laval grew fast after the metro extension in 2007, and much of the city sits on sensitive marine clay that loses strength when disturbed. That history shapes how we approach geophysics here. A VES sounding uses four electrodes—two current, two potential—pushed into the ground in a straight line. We increase the electrode spacing step by step, sending current deeper with each measurement. The apparent resistivity curve tells us where the clay ends and the till or rock begins. In the eastern sectors near the Rivière des Prairies, we often see a perched water table that creates a low-resistivity lens; our field crew has learned to recognize that signature and flag it before excavation begins. For projects where fill thickness is critical, pairing VES with test pit logging gives us ground-truth at the top few meters while the resistivity section extends the profile to 30 meters or more. The equipment is battery-operated, non-invasive, and we can work around existing structures without shutting down the site.

Regional considerations

The Champlain Sea clay that underlies most of Laval has a resistivity below 10 ohm-m, which is our first red flag. When we see that low-resistivity layer extending deep without interruption, we know we are dealing with a thick sequence of sensitive clay—the same material that triggered the Saint-Jean-Vianney landslide upstream. Depth to bedrock varies from 5 meters near the Laurentian foothills to over 30 meters in the central plateau, and missing a shallow rock high by just a few meters can double the cost of pile driving. We have seen cases where an assumed uniform clay profile turned out to have a buried esker—a ridge of sand and gravel—that completely changed the dewatering plan. Electrical resistivity catches those hidden features because sand and gravel are resistive, while clay is conductive. In Laval's Zone 2 seismic category under NBCC, knowing the soil stiffness profile matters for site classification, and VES data feeds directly into that analysis.

Complementary services

01

VES Sounding (1D)

A single vertical profile using the Schlumberger array. We expand electrode spacing progressively to map resistivity versus depth at one location. Ideal for foundation investigations where you need to know clay thickness, bedrock depth, or groundwater level. We deliver an interpreted layer model with resistivity values and estimated unit boundaries.

02

Electrical Resistivity Tomography (2D)

A multi-electrode line that builds a continuous cross-section of the subsurface. We use 48 or 72 electrodes on a cable with automatic switching. This method reveals lateral changes—buried channels, fault zones, or fill boundaries—that a single VES point can miss. Output is a color-coded resistivity section with geotechnical interpretation overlain.

Quick answers

How deep can a VES sounding reach in Laval soils?

With a standard 200-meter cable spread using the Schlumberger array, we typically reach 30 to 40 meters depth. The actual penetration depends on the surface layer resistivity—conductive clay attenuates the signal less than dry sand, so we often get better depth in Laval's marine clay than in granular deposits.

What does a resistivity survey cost for a typical residential lot?

For a single VES sounding on a standard residential lot in Laval, the cost ranges from CA$980 to CA$1,360 depending on access conditions and line length. A 2D tomography line with 48 electrodes runs higher because of the longer field time and inversion processing.

Can you run resistivity lines on asphalt or concrete?

We need electrode contact with the ground, so asphalt and concrete are a problem. We work around paved areas by running lines along grass strips, sidewalks edges, or through the building footprint before the slab is poured. If the site is fully paved, we use a hammer drill to create small contact holes, but it slows the survey down.

How do you calibrate resistivity values to actual soil types?

The reference range for this service in Laval is CA$980 - CA$1.360. The final price depends on the project scope and volume.
