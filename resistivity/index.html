Electrical Resistivity Imaging and VES Sounding in Laval

Together, we solve the challenges of tomorrow.

LEARN MORE →

A few months ago we were called out to a site off Boulevard Saint-Martin. The contractor was ready to dig but the borehole log showed a buried channel of till that didn't match the surrounding clay. We ran a VES line in under two hours and the resistivity profile lit up the channel boundaries clear as day. Laval's subsurface is like that—glacial clay, compact till, and limestone bedrock stacked in unpredictable layers. Electrical resistivity lets us see the contrasts without waiting for lab results. It works because the Champlain Sea clay is conductive while the till and limestone are resistive. When we combine VES soundings with a few SPT boreholes, we anchor the resistivity values to actual blow counts, giving the structural engineer a continuous cross-section instead of just point data.

A resistivity contrast of 10 ohm-m versus 100 ohm-m is often the difference between stable till and sensitive clay in the Champlain Sea basin.

Methodology and scope

Laval grew fast after the metro extension in 2007, and much of the city sits on sensitive marine clay that loses strength when disturbed. That history shapes how we approach geophysics here. A VES sounding uses four electrodes—two current, two potential—pushed into the ground in a straight line. We increase the electrode spacing step by step, sending current deeper with each measurement. The apparent resistivity curve tells us where the clay ends and the till or rock begins. In the eastern sectors near the Rivière des Prairies, we often see a perched water table that creates a low-resistivity lens; our field crew has learned to recognize that signature and flag it before excavation begins. For projects where fill thickness is critical, pairing VES with test pit logging gives us ground-truth at the top few meters while the resistivity section extends the profile to 30 meters or more. The equipment is battery-operated, non-invasive, and we can work around existing structures without shutting down the site.

Local geotechnical context

Beneath much of Laval lies Champlain Sea clay with resistivity under 10 ohm-m, an immediate warning sign. When this low-resistivity layer is continuous at depth, it indicates a thick deposit of sensitive clay—the same material responsible for the upstream Saint-Jean-Vianney landslide. Bedrock depth ranges from 5 m near the Laurentian foothills to over 30 m in the central plateau, and even a few meters' error in locating a shallow rock high can significantly increase pile-driving costs. Instances have occurred where an assumed uniform clay profile actually concealed a buried esker—a sand and gravel ridge—that completely altered the dewatering approach. Electrical resistivity detects these hidden structures because sand and gravel are resistive while clay is conductive. For Laval's seismic Zone 2 under NBCC, site classification relies on soil stiffness, and VES data directly contribute to that evaluation.

Associated technical services

01

VES Sounding (1D)

A point-specific vertical sounding using the Schlumberger electrode array is performed. Electrode spacing is gradually increased to map resistivity changes with depth at that single location. This technique is well-suited for foundation studies requiring knowledge of clay thickness, bedrock depth, or water table elevation. The final deliverable includes an interpreted layered model with resistivity values and estimated boundaries between units.

02

Electrical Resistivity Tomography (2D)

A 2D resistivity tomography line employs 48 or 72 electrodes on a cable with automatic switching to generate a continuous subsurface cross-section. This method captures lateral variations—such as buried channels, fault zones, or fill boundaries—that a single VES point might overlook. The output is a color-coded resistivity section overlaid with geotechnical interpretations.

Regulatory framework

The work follows ASTM D6431-18 (Standard Guide for Using the Direct Current Resistivity Method), NBCC 2020 Division B Part 4 for seismic site classification, and CSA A23.3 Annex D for geotechnical input to foundation design.

Questions and answers

How deep can a VES sounding reach in Laval soils?

The actual depth of investigation depends on surface layer resistivity; conductive clay attenuates the signal less than dry sand, so Laval's marine clay typically yields better depth penetration than granular deposits. With a standard 200-meter electrode cable using the Schlumberger configuration, we commonly achieve 30 to 40 meters depth.

What does a resistivity survey cost for a typical residential lot?

For a single VES sounding on a standard residential lot in Laval, the cost is between CA$980 and CA$1,360, depending on access conditions and cable length. A 48-electrode 2D tomography line costs more due to longer field time and inversion processing.

Can you run resistivity lines on asphalt or concrete?

Proper electrode contact with the ground is essential, so asphalt and concrete pose challenges. To work around paved areas, we run survey lines along grass strips, sidewalk edges, or across the building footprint before the slab is poured. On fully paved sites, a hammer drill creates small contact holes, though this slows progress.

How do you calibrate resistivity values to actual soil types?
