Raft/Mat Foundation Design for Laval’s Complex Glacial Soils

Together, we solve the challenges of tomorrow.

LEARN MORE →

Setting up a total station and watching the drill rig track across a Laval site, you quickly understand why the foundation approach here needs to be different. The stratigraphy across Île Jésus, where Laval sits, is rarely straightforward—you might find a dense till layer at 4 m in one corner of the lot and 12 m of sensitive silty clay in another. Raft/mat foundation design becomes a practical solution when differential settlement is the primary concern, spreading structural loads across a continuous reinforced slab so the building moves as a single unit. The field crew typically logs the first few meters with test pits to expose the crust before deeper sampling, because the weathered top layer often hides the real behavior of the Champlain Sea deposits underneath. We combine those observations with lab consolidation curves and site-specific seismic demands from NBCC 2020 to size the mat thickness, reinforcement layout, and sub-base preparation in a way that fits the real ground conditions, not just a textbook assumption.

In Laval’s sensitive marine clay, a raft foundation isn’t just about bearing capacity—it’s about controlling differential settlement so the structure settles as a monolithic block.

Methodology and scope

Laval’s geotechnical personality is shaped largely by the Champlain Sea clay—a post-glacial marine deposit that can lose significant strength when remolded. The sensitive nature of this clay means that bearing capacity is rarely the limiting factor; instead, total and differential settlement control the design. A typical raft/mat foundation in the Sainte-Dorothée or Chomedey sectors often interacts with a desiccated crust of 1.5 to 3 m thickness, underlain by soft to firm silty clay extending to refusal on till or rock. The modulus of subgrade reaction derived from plate load tests or back-calculated from CPTu data tends to be highly variable, so we avoid generic values and run multiple iterations with different soil-structure interaction models. Reinforcement in the mat is detailed not only for flexure under column loads but also to resist the shrinkage and temperature effects that Quebec’s freeze-thaw cycles impose on exposed slabs. Where the clay is thicker, the design often incorporates a rigid inclusion or stone column support system beneath the raft edge to smooth the stiffness transition between the building footprint and the surrounding grade, reducing the long-term rotation that can affect elevator pits and underground parking ramps.

Local geotechnical context

In Laval, we frequently observe that projects using shallow mat foundations lacking adequate under-slab drainage experience gradual tilting or cracking within five to ten years. This is primarily a water-related issue: either a perched water table in the granular fill weakens the underlying clay, or frost heave under unheated slabs causes differential movement. The Champlain Sea clay can suffer up to 80% reduction in its undisturbed strength when disturbed; therefore, construction activities like excavation instability or open sump pumping may create soft zones that later cause mat settlement. Even a properly designed raft foundation can fail if backfill adjacent to utility trenches is left uncompacted, forming a stiff area surrounded by looser soil. Our current design reports for Laval sites now incorporate specific subgrade protection measures, dewatering cutoff schedules, and compaction verification via sand cone tests, because these construction-phase factors determine whether a mat lasts fifty years or starts tilting before landscaping.

Associated technical services

01

Geotechnical Investigation for Raft Design

Our field program is tailored for Laval sites, using CPTu soundings, thin-wall Shelby tube sampling, and geophysical MASW surveys to define the till depth and clay sensitivity. This investigation follows ASTM D1586 and D5778 standards, yielding consolidation and strength parameters essential for a robust mat foundation analysis.

02

Soil-Structure Interaction and Structural Design

With a modulus of subgrade reaction from site-specific data, we perform finite element modeling of the mat to evaluate column punching shear, flexural moments, and thermal effects. Reinforcement is designed per CSA A23.3, and we work in close coordination with the structural engineer to ensure proper integration with shear walls and elevator cores.

03

Construction-Phase Subgrade Inspection

During excavation and subgrade preparation, we conduct field reviews to confirm that the exposed soil aligns with the design profile. Activities include proof rolling checks, compaction testing of granular fill, and verification that dewatering is operational prior to mud slab placement.

Regulatory framework

The applicable codes and standards include the National Building Code of Canada (NBCC 2020), CSA A23.3-19 for concrete structure design, and ASTM D2435/D2435M-11 for one-dimensional consolidation properties of soils.

Questions and answers

What makes raft/mat foundation design different in Laval compared to other regions of Quebec?

A key distinction for Laval is the extensive occurrence of sensitive Champlain Sea clay on Île Jésus. This marine deposit can suffer significant strength loss upon disturbance, necessitating conservative designs for differential settlement and construction-phase impacts. Additionally, the depth to glacial till varies widely across Laval, so each site demands a comprehensive investigation—extrapolating from adjacent properties is not reliable.

How long does the design process take for a typical mat foundation in Laval?

For a typical commercial or multi-residential mat foundation in Laval, the geotechnical analysis and structural design timeline is three to four weeks from completion of field work. This period covers consolidation settlement analysis, calibration of subgrade reaction modulus, and collaboration with the structural team. Projects with irregular column layouts or deep soft layers may need extra iteration time.

What does a raft/mat foundation design service cost for a Laval project?

Geotechnical investigation and raft foundation design for Laval sites generally cost between CA$1,530 and CA$5,430. The price varies with building footprint, number of CPTu soundings and boreholes, and settlement analysis complexity. A tailored proposal is issued after reviewing architectural loads and existing geotechnical information for the area.

Can a raft/mat foundation be designed without deep boreholes if we have CPTu data?

While CPTu data provides continuous stratigraphic profiling and undrained shear strength estimates, for conclusive raft/mat foundation design in Laval we advise at least one borehole with thin-wall Shelby tube sampling. Oedometer tests on undisturbed samples yield consolidation parameters crucial for determining settlement magnitude and time rate under the mat's sustained loads.
