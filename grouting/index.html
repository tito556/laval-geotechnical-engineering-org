Grouting Design for Ground Improvement and Water Control in Laval

Together, we solve the challenges of tomorrow.

LEARN MORE →

A recent excavation on Boulevard Saint-Martin hit a pocket of loose till at 4 meters, and the inflow started within hours. The contractor had to halt work and dewater for three days before the pit was stable. In Laval, where the stratigraphy jumps from stiff clay to fractured limestone without warning, grouting design is not a generic exercise — it adapts to what the drill rig encounters meter by meter. Our team develops injection programs based on real-time permeability data and the specific fracture patterns of the Chazy and Black River groups. For projects near the Rivière des Prairies, where hydrostatic pressure is higher, we often pair the grouting plan with in-situ permeability testing to calibrate mix rheology before production starts.

A grouting program designed without stage isolation in fractured limestone is just expensive water — and in Laval, that water usually ends up in your excavation.

Methodology and scope

The equipment configuration we specify for Laval jobs typically includes a colloidal mixer and a progressive cavity pump, paired with a data logger that records pressure, flow rate, and volume at 1-second intervals. This setup matters here because the till matrix absorbs water fast, and without continuous monitoring it is easy to lose a batch into an unidentified void. We rely on the GIN (Grouting Intensity Number) method for rock curtains, capping pressure at 30 bar in most residential zones to avoid heave under existing footings. For compaction grouting in granular lenses, we use low-mobility mortar with a slump under 50 mm, pushing it through a 76 mm casing advanced with a track-mounted drill. Real-time pressure-decay analysis lets us stop injection at refusal without fracturing the formation — a critical safeguard when the limestone roof is irregular and only a few meters below a slab-on-grade.
  • Colloidal mixer with continuous recording of pressure, volume, and flow rate
  • GIN-based control for rock curtains below the water table
  • Low-mobility mortar (slump < 50 mm) for compaction grouting in granular pockets
  • Packers set in NQ or HQ boreholes, with double-packer systems for stage grouting

Local considerations

The most frequent mistake we see in Laval is contractors treating grouting as a uniform procedure — same pressure, same mix, same refusal — across an entire site. The till overlying the limestone is not homogeneous: it contains sand lenses, silt seams, and occasional boulders that create preferential flow paths. Injecting a thin cement grout at high pressure into one of these lenses can daylight 15 meters away, or worse, enter a sewer lateral and set in the pipe. We have been called to more than one emergency repair after an uncontrolled injection lifted a garage slab in Sainte-Rose. A proper design segments the treatment zone by stratigraphy, assigns stage lengths based on fracture frequency, and specifies verification holes drilled at 20 percent of the primary spacing. Without that structure, grouting becomes guesswork — and the liability rests entirely with the contractor.

Permeation and Curtain Grouting Design

Frequently asked questions

What is the typical cost range for a grouting design in Laval?

For a standard residential or light commercial project, grouting design fees typically range from CA$1,750 to CA$5,420. The total depends on the number of injection zones, whether Lugeon or Lefranc permeability tests are required for calibration, and the complexity of the verification program.

How do you determine grout mix viscosity for the till in Laval?

Viscosity is selected based on the permeability of the target zone, measured in situ with a falling-head test or a packer test in rock. For tight silty till with permeability below 1×10⁻⁵ cm/s, we specify microfine cements or colloidal silica; for open fractures or clean sand lenses, a stable Type 10 cement grout with a w/c ratio of 0.8:1 to 1.5:1 is usually sufficient.

What verification testing do you specify after grouting?

Our designs require verification holes at a minimum of 10 percent of the primary grid density, with core recovery and Lugeon testing in rock zones, or SPT and permeability tests in overburden. We also specify real-time pressure-volume recording during production injection, so that the refusal criteria are documented for every single point.
