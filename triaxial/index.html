Triaxial Testing for Geotechnical Design Across Laval

Together, we solve the challenges of tomorrow.

LEARN MORE →

Most of Laval sits on a thick blanket of Champlain Sea clay, deposited roughly 10,000 years ago when the island was submerged. The upper crust can be stiff, but just a few meters down the sensitivity jumps dramatically. We have seen boreholes near Rivière des Mille Îles where the undrained shear strength drops to less than 15 kPa in the soft grey silty clay. In these conditions, picking a friction angle from a textbook correlation is a gamble. A proper triaxial test is the only way to extract reliable effective stress parameters, especially when the project involves deep excavations or embankments near the river. Combining this data with a CPT campaign often reveals thin sand lenses within the clay that can act as drainage layers during consolidation.

A single triaxial test on Laval's Champlain clay gives you more design certainty than a dozen empirical correlations from SPT blow counts.

Methodology and scope

The most common mistake we see in Laval is designing shallow footings using drained parameters from a single-stage quick undrained test on a Shelby tube sample. The sample disturbance from tube extraction in this varved clay is significant, and a poorly prepared triaxial test will underestimate the true cohesion and overestimate the stiffness. We always recommend a B-value check exceeding 0.95 before shearing, and we prefer isotropic consolidation for CU tests with pore pressure measurement to properly separate the dilative and contractive behavior. For projects where embankment settlement controls the design, linking the triaxial test to a consolidation study helps define the preconsolidation pressure with much greater confidence than an oedometer alone.

Local geotechnical context

The rapid growth of Laval from a rural parish to a suburban center with 450,000 inhabitants meant that much of the older infrastructure near Chomedey and Pont-Viau was constructed before modern triaxial testing was widespread. Our investigations at sites where the 1960s design assumed a drained friction angle of 32° for the clay crust revealed that CU tests indicate an effective friction angle closer to 26°, along with a small cohesion intercept that diminishes with strain. The typical risk scenario involves differential settlement in buildings on spread footings because the designer relied on peak strength without recognizing that Champlain clay loses structure at axial strains below 2%. For retaining walls along service roads, the lateral earth pressure distribution changes entirely once the true effective stress path is known.

Associated technical services

01

Consolidated Undrained (CU) with Pore Pressure

For Laval clay, the standard approach involves measuring excess pore pressure during shear to establish the effective stress envelope (c' and φ'). This is crucial for slope stability assessments and staged construction projects.

02

Unconsolidated Undrained (UU) Testing

These tests are employed for short-term bearing capacity evaluations on intact clay samples. They are conducted promptly after extrusion to measure the in-situ undrained shear strength before swelling modifies the clay structure.

03

Consolidated Drained (CD) Testing

This method is applied to the sandy till lenses located below the clay in northern Laval. The slow strain rate ensures complete drainage, providing the actual drained friction angle for long-term foundation design.

Regulatory framework

The relevant ASTM standards include D4767-11 for CU testing with pore pressure measurement, D2850-15 for UU triaxial compression, and D7181-20 for CD triaxial compression.

Questions and answers

Why can't I just use SPT N-values instead of running a triaxial test in Laval?

SPT correlations for shear strength are primarily calibrated for sands and stiff clays in other regions. In Laval, the sensitive Champlain clay may yield a reasonable N-value, yet it can fail at low strain because of its meta-stable structure. Direct measurement of the stress-strain curve via a triaxial test reveals the collapse behavior that an N-value fails to capture.

How much does a triaxial test program cost in Laval?

The cost for a comprehensive triaxial program—comprising three CU specimens with pore pressure measurement and a full report—typically falls between CA$2,620 and CA$4,090, depending on specimen count and consolidation stages. Pricing is always project-specific after examining the borehole logs.

What sample quality do you need for a reliable triaxial test?

Undisturbed Shelby tube samples are required, with a minimum diameter of 75 mm, and must be sealed with wax right after field extrusion. Transport should be vertical and free of vibration. For CD tests on sand lenses, frozen samples or block samples from test pits are occasionally used to maintain fabric integrity.

How long does it take to get triaxial test results?

The duration of a standard CU triaxial test with back-pressure saturation ranges from 7 to 10 days from sample setup to final report. CD tests on fine-grained soils require more time—up to two weeks—due to the slow drained strain rate. However, preliminary parameters can be supplied sooner if the project timeline is urgent.

Can you model the stress path for an excavation near the Rivière des Prairies?

Absolutely. Our stress-path triaxial tests involve reducing the confining pressure while maintaining the axial load, simulating lateral unloading during excavation. This approach provides a far more realistic stiffness and strength assessment for excavation sidewalls compared to a standard compression test.
