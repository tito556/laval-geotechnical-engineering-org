In-Situ Testing in Laval

Together, we solve the challenges of tomorrow.

LEARN MORE →

Within Laval, dependable geotechnical investigations rely heavily on in-situ testing, which yields direct property measurements of soil and rock while avoiding the disruptions typical of sampling and subsequent lab work. A variety of field techniques fall under this category, aimed at assessing strength, compressibility, permeability, and stratigraphy in real site conditions. On Île Jésus, where subsurface variations can be pronounced over short distances, borehole logs and laboratory analyses alone frequently fall short. In-situ methods fill this void by providing continuous or depth-specific information, enabling engineers to create safer foundation designs, manage groundwater, and lower hazards related to the area's sensitive clay deposits.

Laval's geology is dominated by the Champlain Sea clay, a marine deposit known for its high sensitivity, low shear strength, and potential for retrogressive landslides. Overlying till or bedrock, this clay is interspersed with sand lenses and underlain by sedimentary rocks of the Lorraine and Utica groups. The presence of a shallow water table across much of the island adds complexity, making accurate assessment of hydrogeological parameters critical. A standard field permeability test (Lefranc/Lugeon) becomes essential here, not just for dewatering design but for understanding the long-term settlement behavior of the sensitive clays. These local conditions demand testing methods that can capture the nuanced behavior of the soil without causing remolding or sample disturbance.

In Laval, every in-situ testing procedure must comply with the demanding specifications of the Code de construction du Québec and the national standard CAN/BNQ 2501-135, which incorporates ASTM and ISO methods but with certain regional adaptations. The relevant norm for geotechnical investigations is CAN/BNQ 2501-130, setting minimal investigation criteria depending on a building's significance and its seismic classification. Furthermore, the Règlement sur les carrières et sablières dictates testing requirements close to aggregate extraction operations, which are widespread in the northern part of the region. Following these standards is not merely a recommended practice; it is a legal mandate enforced by the Régie du bâtiment du Québec, guaranteeing that field data is defensible, reproducible, and appropriate for the advanced analytical models now commonly used locally.

Different in-situ testing approaches are needed for projects across Laval's diverse terrain. In the Pont-Viau and Chomedey districts, high-density residential towers utilize deep cone penetration testing (CPT) combined with pore pressure measurements to characterize the sensitive clay and evaluate liquefaction risks. Infrastructure projects, like extensions to the Société de transport de Laval network, depend on pressuremeter tests to derive deformation moduli essential for tunnel and deep excavation designs. For numerous commercial developments built on what was formerly farmland, vane shear testing supplies undrained shear strength values crucial for slope stability analysis. Even construction of single-family homes in areas such as Sainte-Rose gains from dynamic cone penetration testing to confirm bearing capacity prior to foundation design.

Questions and answers

Why is in-situ testing preferred over laboratory testing for Laval's sensitive clays?

Sensitive Champlain Sea clay samples subjected to laboratory testing frequently experience disturbance during collection, transportation, and extrusion, resulting in shear strengths that are considerably underestimated and consolidation parameters that are deceptive. Field vane shear tests or CPT, being in-situ, assess properties without such disruption, maintaining the native soil structure and cementation bonds. Hence they offer a more faithful depiction of actual in-situ behavior, which is vital for evaluating slope stability and designing foundations in Laval.

What local regulations govern the depth and frequency of in-situ tests in Laval?

Adopted by the Quebec Construction Code, the CAN/BNQ 2501-130 standard defines minimal investigation depths according to foundation type and building footprint. For a standard mid-rise building in Laval, boreholes combined with in-situ testing should reach a depth at which the stress increase drops below 10% of the effective overburden pressure, or else extend down to competent bedrock. Additionally, the standard requires at least one investigation point per 300 m² for buildings, with extra testing mandated where sensitive clay or variable stratigraphy is suspected.

How does the shallow water table in Laval affect the selection of in-situ testing methods?

Across most of Laval, the groundwater table is high, frequently less than 2 meters below the surface, which makes pore pressure measurement a necessity during all penetration testing. It is strongly advised to use a piezocone (CPTu) rather than a standard CPT in order to capture the dissipation characteristics required for consolidation analysis. When evaluating permeability, a field permeability test (Lefranc/Lugeon) must be performed at several depths to account for the stratified deposits, because sand lenses in the clay can form preferential flow paths that markedly change the site's overall hydrogeological behavior.

What is the typical scope of an in-situ testing campaign for a commercial development in Laval?

For a commercial project, a typical investigation campaign starts with a desk study reviewing surficial geology maps and previous landslide records. During the field phase, boreholes are combined with standard penetration testing (SPT) at regular intervals, along with CPTu soundings to delineate stratigraphy and pinpoint sensitive clay layers. Undrained strength is measured via vane shear tests in the clay, and pressuremeter tests may be included for deep foundation design. If permanent dewatering is intended or the excavation goes beneath the water table, a field permeability test becomes essential.
